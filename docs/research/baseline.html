Baseline: where the cost goes

Issue: #19 · Date: 2026-10-19 · Status: complete

Question​

After a week of ordinary use through Janus, where does the upstream cost actually go: by request class, by kind of token, by session? And what does that mean for the charter's G1 target, a cost per working session at least 30% below the baseline?

Decision​

  1. The baseline for G1 is $23.20 per working session (median), $39.81 (mean), at list price. A working session is one Claude Code session id with at least 20 main-loop turns; seven of the eight sessions were, and they hold all of the cost.
  2. G1's 30% cannot come from the mechanisms Janus has. The cost is the main conversation reading its own cache. Everything Janus can route, escalate, digest, or prune together is under 3% of it here (below), and the charter's cache rule (R1) is why: the context already sent cannot be made smaller without paying to write it again. G1 is restated in the charter: its target is now the saving the data shows is in reach, reported per session, not 30%.
  3. The one large saving in Janus's reach is the cache expiring while you are away. Five idle gaps of 1 to 3 hours cost $20.34 in cache writes, 7% of the week; a cached read every hour of the gap, to keep the cache alive, would have cost about $0.85. That is a spike, #334, not a feature yet: it means Janus sending requests of its own.
  4. The other large lever is how big a session's context grows, and it belongs to the user. Turns above 600k tokens are 13% of turns and 33% of the main conversation's cost. Janus can show it as it happens, and say when a /compact or a fresh session would pay: #335.
  5. Routing subagents to the local model stays an availability feature, not a saving. Subagents were 0.4% of the week's cost. #28, which was to prove a saving from them, cannot: its outcome is in its own comments.

Method​

The ledger from 2026-10-12 05:39 to 2026-10-19 07:25 (local time): 3,222 requests, of which 3,060 were upstream Messages requests with usage, from Janus v0.9.0-rc.4 to rc.17 and Claude Code 2.1.29x to 2.1.303. Costs are recomputed from each record's usage at the list prices in internal/ledger/pricing.go (as of 2026-10-06), splitting cache writes by their 5-minute and 1-hour parts; the total, $279.10, is within 0.3% of janus stats ($278.23). Sessions are grouped by the ledger's session, Claude Code's session id. The figures were computed by a script over the ledger's counts; no captured content was read for them.

Five of the seven days had work on them; there was none on 10-15 or 10-18.

Idle gaps​

Six times, more than an hour passed between turns, the cache expired, and the next turn wrote the conversation again:

GapContextCache writeOne cached read of it
1.2 h116k$0.92$0.023

2.5 h946k$7.57$0.189

A read refreshes the cache's lifetime, so one read an hour keeps it. For the five gaps under three hours, that is about $0.85 instead of $20.34. Over the 38.8-hour gap, reading every hour would have cost as much as the write it saved, so keeping a cache warm only pays for a few hours.

What Janus can reach​

MechanismCeiling hereSource
Route or escalate subagents, status, compaction0.9%this note

Keep the cache warm through idle gapsabout 7%this note, #334

Auto mode's safety checks (10% where seen) are not on the list: they are a security decision, and sending them to a local model would weaken it.

What this does not show​

  • One person, one week, one way of working. Most of the work was Janus's own development, in long sessions, with no subagents asked for. Someone who uses subagents heavily would see routing matter more.
  • 10-13 includes evaluation sessions. The digest evaluation (digest.md) ran that day, through Janus.
  • Auto mode's safety checks stopped appearing after 2026-10-14 16:50, though auto mode was in use on 10-19. Either they no longer pass through ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL, or they take a shape Janus does not recognise. Which, the ledger cannot say.
  • List prices, not a bill. The owner's plan is a subscription; the dollars measure usage, and the shares are what matters.
  • Task success was not measured. Nothing here says whether a cheaper session would have done the work as well.
